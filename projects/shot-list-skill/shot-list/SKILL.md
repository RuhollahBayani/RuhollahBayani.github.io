# Shot List

Turn a client brief into a shoot plan a photographer can work from on set.

## 1. Read the brief

Pull out: subject (person, product, both), purpose and where it will be used (Instagram, print, e-commerce, campaign), mood and references, location or studio, how much time is booked, and how many final images are needed.

If something essential is missing, don't stop to ask. Make a sensible assumption, list it under **Assumptions** at the top, and carry on. Only ask first if you can't tell what is being photographed.

## 2. Write the plan

Use exactly these sections, in this order.

### Overview
Three to five lines: the concept in one sentence, the mood, the deliverables (number of final images, sizes and aspect ratios), and the session length.

### Assumptions
Bullet list. Leave the section out if there are none.

### Shot list
A table with these columns:

| # | Shot | Framing & lens | Light setup | Direction | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Shot:** what is in the frame, in a few words.
- **Framing & lens:** shot size and angle, plus a focal length (e.g. "Tight headshot, eye level, 85mm").
- **Light setup:** a letter referring to the Light setups section (A, B, C…).
- **Direction:** pose, expression or styling notes for the model, or placement and props for products.
- **Priority:** `Must` for shots the deliverables depend on, `Nice` for extras if time allows.

Order the shots so all shots under one lighting setup are together, to cut down on changeovers. Plan about 20% more shots than the deliverables need.

### Light setups
One short entry per setup: key light (modifier, position, height), fill or reflector, rim or background light, and background. Keep it to what fits in the booked studio or location.

### Schedule
Time blocks from arrival to wrap: setup, each lighting block, wardrobe or product changes, and a short buffer before wrap. Make it add up to the booked time.

### Retouch & grade plan
Per look: skin approach (keep texture, how much clean-up), colour grade direction with a reference point (e.g. "cool shadows, warm skin, lifted blacks"), any composite or background work, and export sizes per platform:

- Instagram feed: 1080 × 1350 (4:5)
- Instagram story or reel cover: 1080 × 1920 (9:16)
- Web: 2400px on the long edge

### Gear checklist
Grouped checklist: camera and lenses, lighting and modifiers, grip and stands, backgrounds and props, and tethering, cards and batteries.

### Optional video
Only if the brief mentions video, reels or motion: up to five short clips, each with duration, frame rate (e.g. 24fps, or 60fps for slow motion), and movement.

## Style

- Practical and specific. Name focal lengths, modifiers and distances; avoid vague words like "nice light".
- Short lines that are easy to read on a phone on set.
- No filler intro or sign-off. Start with the Overview.
